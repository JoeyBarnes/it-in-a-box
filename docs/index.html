IT-in-a-Box

A complete model of an enterprise technology function — every capability a business needs in order to operate, expressed as a reusable standard rather than as any one company's organisation chart.It exists to make a recurring assessment repeatable. A business joining a portfolio arrives with a technology function already in place — its own platforms, vendors and decisions — so the question is rarely what is missing. It is how that estate compares to the standard, where convergence is worth the cost, and where the business is better left to run its own. Doing that from a blank page each time is slow and produces answers that cannot be compared across the portfolio. Each capability is mapped to the Microsoft platform, so what is already owned and what still has to be bought fall out of the same view.

Every functional area on one page. Cyber security runs the full height because it qualifies every layer rather than occupying one of them, and governance and business management do the same on the right. The four areas across the top are what the business actually touches; everything beneath them is what makes those work. The bar under each area shows its Microsoft coverage mix. Every Microsoft product the model names, sized by how many capabilities it answers. Workloads Microsoft positions as part of a bigger platform are rolled into it, so Purview counts once rather than five times and Power BI sits inside Fabric. Grouping follows how Microsoft architects these products, not how they are billed — a rolled-up workload may still be a separate purchase, which is why licensing is shown as a spread on each product rather than a single badge. Most products do exactly one job. The handful that do several are where one platform is already earning its keep across the estate, worth knowing before buying something else to do the same work. Hollow circles contribute to a capability without being the named answer.

At roughly this size, what does a business need, what carries it, what would it cost in shape rather than price, and where would building beat buying. In practice this is a longer conversation than any page can hold.
